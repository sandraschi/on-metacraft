## The guild's defense

Every monopoly on making things has defended itself the same way when a
machine arrived: the machine's output is inferior. The claim is usually
true on the first day and false within a decade. The interesting question
is never whether the claim is true but how long it stays true, and what
the incumbent does with the time.

The pattern has six dated instances worth knowing.

## Lyon, 1804

Joseph Marie Jacquard's loom controlled the lifting of warp threads with
a chain of punched cards `[construction: patent and demonstration date,
1801-1804]`. Before it, a figured silk required a drawboy perched on the
loom, pulling cords in sequence for hours. The pattern lived in the
drawboy's attention. After it, the pattern lived in a stack of cards that
could be copied, stored and run again.

The weavers of Lyon resisted; looms were reportedly burned. The
resistance failed for a reason that recurs in this book: the cards were
cheaper to check than the drawboy. A card either has a hole or it does
not. The pattern became data, and data can be verified.

## Highland Park, 1913

Ford's moving assembly line did not invent the automobile. It removed the
coachbuilder's monopoly on assembling one. Interchangeable parts, made to
tolerances a gauge could check, let unskilled workers do in minutes what
had taken a craftsman hours `[construction: Model T chassis assembly time
before/after, 1913-1914]`. Again the enabling condition was cheap
verification: a part fits the gauge or it does not.

## Cambridge, Massachusetts, 1983

On 1983-09-27 Richard Stallman announced the GNU project, a free
reimplementation of Unix. On 1991-08-25 Linus Torvalds announced a kernel
to go with it. Together they broke the licensing monopoly on Unix, not by
lawsuit but by rewriting it, interface by interface, against a published
standard (POSIX) and an observable original.

This is the closest historical precedent to ArtCraft, and it is also a
warning. GNU/Linux won the server and lost the desktop. It reimplemented
the interface programs used, not the interface people used. Chapter III
returns to that difference; it is the hinge of the book.

## Toronto and San Diego, 1986

Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams published backpropagation in *Nature* on
1986-10-09. It took until 2012, and a GPU, for the method to beat
hand-built feature engineering decisively (AlexNet, ImageNet 2012). The
monopoly broken was that of the expert who wrote the rules by hand. The
mechanism was statistical learning from data the expert did not curate.

## Santa Clara, 2006

NVIDIA's CUDA `[construction: release date, 2006-11 or 2007-02]` turned a
graphics card into a general matrix engine. It broke the dominance of
scalar general-purpose processors for numerical work. Without it, neither
the 2012 result nor the 2026 agent swarms exist.

## San Francisco, 2026

The sixth mechanizer has no single name. It is a method: a parity
document scored against the installed original, a reference
implementation the agents can test against, a budget in agent-hours, and
a swarm of model instances writing code in parallel. Chapter I dates its
public arrival to 2026-09-30.

The monopoly it breaks is the labor cost of reimplementation. The guild
defense has already been mounted: the clones are buggy, incomplete, alpha.
All three are true on day thirteen.

## A counter-case: Bessemer

Not every mechanizer was robbed. Henry Bessemer patented his converter
process in 1855-1856, licensed it, and is estimated to have earned over
a million pounds in royalties before the patent expired in 1870. When
William Kelly's rival American patent appeared, it was bought and pooled
with Bessemer's; mills paid under both. The law worked as designed.

Bessemer matters here because he is the exception that shows the rule's
real shape: patents protected a physical process with a twenty-year
clock. The clones of Chapter I attack software whose core is older than
twenty years. Chapter III takes up the difference.

## The editorial correction

The Gemini draft listed Dario Amodei as a mechanizer, for constitutional
scaffolding of frontier models. That is a brake, not a mechanizer: it
constrains a machine; it breaks no guild. It moves to Chapter VI as an
attachment point.
