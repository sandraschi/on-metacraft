# Preface

**Status:** Draft v0.1 2026-10-13 (Opus 5.5). Gemini and Sandra review pending.
**Revision date:** 2026-10-13.

This book was started on 2026-10-13, thirteen days after the first commit
to a repository called `storytold/photocraft`. By the time the first draft
existed, the same organization had published thirteen desktop applications,
each aimed at a named incumbent, each released roughly once a day. The book
is therefore written from inside the event it describes. Some of its dates
will be wrong by the time anyone reads it. Chapter V exists to absorb that:
it is a living ledger, revised in place and dated at every revision.

## Two levels of authorship

The first draft of the argument came from Gemini, in a chat chain, on
2026-10-13. It is preserved word for word in Side - The Gemini First Draft,
in this book.
Claude Opus 5.5 reviewed it the same day, checked its claims against public
repositories and press, and disagreed with parts of it. Those reviews are
kept as dated side chapters (Sides - Reviews A-F), never edited after their date.
Sandra Schipal judges between the models and owns the text.

This arrangement is not decoration. A book about software built by agent
swarms, drafted by competing models and judged by one human, is a small
instance of its own subject. Where a model's judgment survives into the
text, the commit carries a `Co-Authored-By:` trailer.

## What this book claims

Proprietary desktop software was defended less by law, and less by
genius, than by the labor cost of reimplementing it. In 2026, agent
harnesses running on cheap compute collapsed that cost. The law had
permitted clean-room reimplementation for decades. What remains of the
moat after the collapse is switching cost, distribution, certification,
liability, and a narrow, decaying band of patents.

## What this book does not do

- It does not give legal advice. Where it discusses doctrine, it cites
  the case and the date. Ask counsel.
- It does not give investment advice. Chapter VI separates exposed
  vendors from defended ones; that is analysis, not a trade.
- It does not teach offensive technique. The same harness that clones a
  photo editor can be pointed at contested domains. The book names those
  domains once, in Side - The Dark Mirror, at the level of institutions and
  governance only (Rule 2, `CONTRIBUTING.md`).

## How to read it

Chapters I-V establish what happened and when. Chapter VI lists what still
holds and who holds it. Chapter VII is about the compute that pays for the
collapse. Chapters VIII and IX follow the method off the screen and up the
autonomy ladder. Chapter X sets the physical limits.

Every unverified claim carries a `[construction]` flag. A chapter with no
flags left is a finished chapter. At 2026-10-13 there are none.
